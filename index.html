I'm a backend engineer. I build services that stay fast and correct under real load — and I measure both.

Most of my work lives between the database and the user: REST and WebSocket APIs in Node.js and FastAPI, data models in Postgres and MongoDB, and the caching, concurrency and model-loading decisions that decide whether a response takes 200 milliseconds or 20 seconds. I profile before I optimize, parallelize anything that's independent, and keep expensive work off the hot path.

I care just as much about correctness at the edges: idempotent writes, safe retries, duplicate-proof flows, and test suites that catch regressions before users do. Lately that includes production LLM systems — retrieval, vector search and agent pipelines with real guardrails around them.

An autonomous research agent. It breaks a question into sub-queries, searches arXiv, Semantic Scholar, DBLP and Crossref in parallel, extracts the claims each paper makes, and detects where sources contradict each other. It's built as a checkpointed LangGraph state machine that fans out retrieval, extraction and contradiction checks across workers, which cut a full synthesis from 6.5 minutes to about 70 seconds. A writer/critic loop retracts any claim that fails a citation whitelist, and cost accounting stays thread-safe across parallel runs.
